The feedback loop the only thing that proves it's working
Weigh 3–4 mornings a week. After the bathroom, before food. Use the weekly average, never a single day.
Target: +0.3 kg per week. Roughly 1.3 kg a month. Slow is correct — faster than this is mostly fat.
Average flat for 2 weeks? Add +250 kcal per day and re-check. Repeat until it moves.
Gaining over 0.5 kg/week? Drop 200 kcal. You're outrunning what your body can build.
Scale stuck but lifts going up? Keep going, don't touch anything. Weight follows strength.
Supplements two worth buying
Whey protein concentrate — ~24 g protein and ~120 kcal a scoop, drinkable when you can't be bothered to eat. Concentrate over isolate even with money to spare: isolate is just concentrate with the carbs and fat filtered out, and those are calories you're actively chasing. Same muscle, less of what you need. Only switch if lactose upsets your stomach.
Creatine monohydrate, 5 g/day — the only other supplement with real evidence behind it. More reps per set, a couple of kg on the scale within a month. No loading phase, timing doesn't matter, take it on rest days too.
Mass gainers — no, and not because of the price. A serving is mostly maltodextrin: a very fast carb that spikes blood sugar, upsets a lot of stomachs at a full scoop, and flattens your appetite for the next real meal. The smoothie below hits the same calories from oats, milk and peanut butter, which digest slower and carry actual nutrients. Keep a tub for travel if you want; don't build the plan on it.
Nothing else is worth buying. Not BCAAs, not glutamine, not test boosters. Past food and creatine, money stops buying muscle — if you have budget spare, spend it on a second smoothie a day.

If you're still not gaining
The problem is never metabolism. It's that you ate less than you think. Track one honest day — weigh everything — and you'll find the gap.
Drink calories instead of chewing them. A second smoothie is easier than a second meal.
Add fat to what you already eat: olive oil on rice, extra peanut butter, full-fat milk over skimmed. 300 kcal with no extra volume in your stomach.
Progress is a spreadsheet, not a feeling. Log your lifts.
